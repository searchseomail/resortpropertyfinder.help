I bought a place in Bansko last year. For a decent one-bedroom unit within walking distance to the gondola, you should budget between €45,000 and €65,000. Don't forget to factor in the notary fees, local taxes, and agent commissions, which usually add about 5-7% to the purchase price.

The legal process for foreigners is fairly straightforward, but you will need a local lawyer to conduct due diligence. A key step is getting your finances in order first; check out this guide on opening a Bulgarian bank account to ensure your funds are ready for the transfer.

Maintenance fees are the biggest 'hidden' cost. In many complexes, expect to pay between €8 and €12 per square meter annually. Make sure you check if the complex has a functioning spa and pool, as those drive the fees up significantly.

If you're looking for investment potential, the rental market is quite seasonal. Bansko is booming in winter, but the summer months are quieter. Some people prefer the coast for year-round rental yields; you might want to compare it with the best coastal towns in Bulgaria before locking yourself into a mountain property.

Always ask for the 'Act 16' document before signing anything. This is the occupancy permit. Without it, you might face issues with utility bills, which are significantly higher for commercial properties than for residential ones.
