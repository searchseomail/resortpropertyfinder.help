Never pay a deposit directly to a broker; always use an escrow account or pay the seller directly after your lawyer performs a thorough 'check for encumbrances' at the Registry Agency. If they push for a quick cash transfer, run away.

Check the property's status via a Certificate of Encumbrances. If the seller is hesitant to provide the document number or claims it's 'too much trouble,' it is almost certainly a scam. Also, compare your potential purchase against this ROI comparison: Seaside apartments vs ski apartments in Bulgaria to see if the price aligns with actual market data.

Legitimate brokers in Bulgaria are happy to show their company registration and provide references. If they don't have a physical office in Bansko or seem to be working from a burner phone, avoid them entirely.

Double-selling is a real risk in developments that haven't been properly registered. Make sure you use an independent lawyer who has no affiliation with the developer or the broker to verify the title deed.

Always ask for the 'Act 16' document, which certifies the building is fit for habitation. Many 'cheap' apartments in Bansko are in buildings that never received this, meaning you will face massive utility bills and legal nightmares.
