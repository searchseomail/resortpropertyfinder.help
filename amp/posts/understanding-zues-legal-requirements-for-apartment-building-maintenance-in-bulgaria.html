Understanding ZUES: Legal requirements for apartment building maintenance in Bulgaria

lost_in_syntax OP
27.01.2025 06:01
I am currently living in a condominium in Sofia and we are struggling to organize repairs for our building's common areas. I am trying to understand the specific obligations of the General Assembly and the building manager under the Bulgarian Condominium Ownership Management Act (ZUES). Could someone clarify how the law mandates the collection of maintenance fees and what the legal process is for enforcing repairs when some owners refuse to contribute? Any advice on navigating these regulations would be greatly appreciated.

Under ZUES, the General Assembly is the supreme decision-making body. If you have non-paying neighbors, the manager must draft a formal protocol of the meeting where the fees were decided and then file for a writ of execution through a lawyer. It's a bureaucratic process but legally sound.

17.02.2025 11:18
The law requires the manager to keep a 'Book of the Condominium.' If someone refuses to pay, you can actually obtain an order for immediate execution under Article 410 of the Civil Procedure Code. It is much easier to manage these things if you have vetted your building manager properly, much like you would when researching new construction in Sofia.

I've been through this in my building in Lozenets. The key is transparency. Ensure the monthly fees are clearly recorded in the minutes of the General Assembly. If owners don't pay, the manager is legally obligated to pursue them, or the manager themselves could be held liable by the other owners.
