Guide to Reselling Property in Bulgaria: Marketing, Paperwork, and Agent Fees

lunar_orbit
Topic Starter
Posted: 18.02.2025 00:42
I am planning to put my apartment in Bansko on the market after owning it for five years, but I am unsure about the current legal requirements for foreign sellers. Could anyone advise on the essential paperwork needed for a smooth transaction and how to identify a reputable real estate agent who charges fair commission rates? Additionally, I would appreciate tips on the best platforms to list my property to reach international buyers effectively. Any advice on navigating the tax implications and notary process would be greatly appreciated.

Selling in Bansko is fairly straightforward after five years. You will need your Notary Deed, a current Tax Assessment (danuchna otsenka), and a Certificate for Lack of Encumbrances. Before you start, definitely look at how to verify property title and check for debts or mortgages in Bulgaria to ensure everything is clean for the buyer.

Replied: 04.03.2025 16:31
I sold my place in Bansko last year. Most reputable agents charge between 2.5% and 3% commission. Always ask for a copy of their company registration and check if they have a physical office in town. Avoid anyone asking for fees upfront.

For international exposure, definitely list on Rightmove Overseas and Gate-Away. These sites attract the British and Western European buyers who are currently looking for ski apartments. Make sure your photos are high-quality and taken during the summer to show the greenery too.

Since you've owned it for five years, you might be exempt from capital gains tax, but check with a local accountant. The notary process is mandatory in Bulgaria; the notary will verify all documents and ensure the transfer is legal. The buyer usually picks the notary, but you can negotiate this.

Don't forget to get an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) before listing; it's a legal requirement now. Also, clear all your utility bills (electricity, water, and maintenance fees) because the notary will require a 'certificate of no outstanding debts' from the building management.

I recommend hiring a lawyer to handle the Preliminary Contract. Even if you use an agent, having an independent legal expert review the paperwork protects you from hidden clauses. It usually costs a few hundred euros but is worth the peace of mind.

Finding a good agent in Bansko can be tricky because there are so many. Look for agencies that are members of the National Real Estate Association (NREA). They have a code of conduct that helps ensure fair play regarding commissions and transparency.
