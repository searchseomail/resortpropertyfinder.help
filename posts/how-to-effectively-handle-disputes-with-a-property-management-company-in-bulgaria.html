How to effectively handle disputes with a property management company in Bulgaria?

golden_ratio_x
Topic Starter
Posted: 12.01.2025 04:31
I am currently living in a gated residential complex in Sofia and we are having serious issues with our building management company regarding maintenance fees and the quality of common area services. Despite our repeated requests for transparent financial reports and repairs, the management remains unresponsive and continues to ignore our collective complaints. What are the legal steps we can take as an owners' association to hold them accountable under the Bulgarian Condominium Ownership Management Act? Any advice on how to initiate a formal dispute or terminate our contract would be greatly appreciated.

The first step is to check if your building is officially registered in the Condominium Register (Етажна собственост). If they aren't providing reports, you need to call a General Assembly meeting. If you are new to the local system, you might find this guide on buying property in Bulgaria helpful for understanding your rights as an owner.
silent_echo
Replied: 07.05.2025 06:32
You should consult the Condominium Ownership Management Act (ZUES). If the management company refuses to show invoices, you have the right to demand an audit. If you are worried about hidden financial issues, always remember the importance of how to verify property title and check for debts before escalating further.
shadow_weaver
Replied: 25.07.2025 16:39
Document everything. Send formal written notices via registered mail (обратна разписка). Verbal complaints mean nothing in Bulgarian courts. If they continue to ignore the General Assembly's decisions, you can terminate the contract based on breach of duty.
copper_fern
Replied: 21.08.2025 02:58
You need to gather at least 20% of the owners to request an extraordinary General Assembly. If the manager refuses to call one, the initiative committee can call it themselves. Make sure the minutes are properly recorded by a clerk.
terra_nova_x
Replied: 22.09.2025 13:58
If the management company is unresponsive, you can file a complaint with the local municipality (Obshtina). They have the authority to impose fines on managers who fail to fulfill their obligations under the law.
urban_nomad
Replied: 26.11.2025 13:18
Collective action is your only leverage. Get a lawyer to draft a formal 'Notice of Termination' citing specific articles of your contract and the ZUES law. Don't stop paying the fees, though, as that could put you in breach; instead, pay them into a separate escrow account if legally advised.
midnight_voyager
Replied: 20.12.2025 14:00
Many of these management companies rely on owners not knowing the law. Once you show them you are organized and have legal representation, they often become much more cooperative. It's often easier than dealing with the complications of new build developments vs resale apartments, but it still requires diligence.
cinder_heart
Replied: 09.01.2026 12:10
Check your initial contract carefully. Most management contracts have a notice period for termination. Ensure your General Assembly votes specifically to terminate the current contract and elect a new professional manager or switch to self-management.
lunar_orbit
Replied: 02.02.2026 05:13
If they aren't providing transparent financial reports, report them to the National Revenue Agency (NAP). Mismanagement of funds is a serious offense in Bulgaria and often forces them to comply once they realize the tax authorities are involved.
