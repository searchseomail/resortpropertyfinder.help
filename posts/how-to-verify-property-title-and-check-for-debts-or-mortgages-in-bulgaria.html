How to verify property title and check for debts or mortgages in Bulgaria

lunar_orbit
Topic Starter
Posted: 15.01.2025 06:23
I am currently looking to purchase a vacation apartment in the Bansko area and I am concerned about potential hidden liabilities. Could anyone explain the process for checking the Property Register for existing mortgages or encumbrances? Is it possible for a foreign buyer to perform these checks independently, or is it mandatory to hire a Bulgarian lawyer to request a Certificate of Encumbrances? Any tips on what specific documents I should request from the seller to ensure the title is clean would be greatly appreciated.

Always start by requesting a 'Certificate of Encumbrances' (Tezhesti) from the Registry Agency. It is the only official document that will show if the property is mortgaged or under a distraint.
urban_nomad
Replied: 03.06.2025 07:20
As a foreigner, you technically can request documents yourself, but the language barrier and bureaucracy make it very difficult. I highly recommend hiring a local lawyer who knows how to navigate the Bulgarian Notary Deed process to avoid any nasty surprises.
silver_lining_8
Replied: 11.06.2025 15:21
Don't forget to ask for the 'Act 16' document, which confirms the building is legally habitable. Without it, you might face issues with utility connections or future insurance.
urban_scribe
Replied: 13.06.2025 16:06
Check the property tax status at the local municipality. If the seller hasn't paid their annual property taxes, the debt can sometimes be tied to the property itself.

Always verify the identity of the seller against the title deed. Ask for a copy of the Notarial Deed (Notarialen Akt) early on to confirm they are indeed the legal owner.

Make sure you account for all the extra costs like notary fees, local taxes, and lawyer commissions. It is easy to underestimate the budget, so read up on unexpected expenses when buying property in Bulgaria.

I bought in Bansko last year and the best advice I can give is to never rely solely on the seller's word. Get a lawyer to perform an independent title search; it costs very little compared to the price of the apartment.

Ask the seller for a 'Certificate of No Tax Obligations' from the National Revenue Agency. This proves they don't have outstanding debts to the state that could lead to a seizure of the property.

If the property is part of a gated complex, request a certificate from the property management company confirming that there are no outstanding maintenance fees (service charges).

The Registry Agency in Bulgaria is public, but the system isn't very foreigner-friendly. Unless you speak fluent Bulgarian, you will likely get lost in the paperwork.
