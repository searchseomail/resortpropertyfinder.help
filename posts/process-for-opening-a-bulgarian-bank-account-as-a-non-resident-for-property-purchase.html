Process for opening a Bulgarian bank account as a non-resident for property purchase

zenith_seeker
Topic Starter
Posted: 21.01.2025 10:40
I am currently in the process of buying a vacation property in Bulgaria and have been told I need a local bank account to handle the utility payments and property tax transfers. As a non-resident, I am finding the documentation requirements to be quite confusing and inconsistent across different banks. Could anyone share their recent experiences or advise on which banks are more foreigner-friendly for this specific purpose? I would appreciate any tips on the necessary paperwork or if I should look into using a local lawyer to facilitate the process.

I went through this last year. UniCredit was the most straightforward for me as a non-resident. Make sure you have your preliminary contract for the property purchase, as they often ask for proof of why you need the account.

It is definitely confusing. I highly recommend using a local lawyer. They can often set up a power of attorney and handle the bank interactions for you, which saves a lot of headaches. Check out this complete step-by-step guide for foreigners buying property.

Avoid the big city branches if you can. I found that branches in smaller towns or near vacation resorts are much more accustomed to dealing with foreigners buying holiday homes.

Don't forget to ask about the ongoing maintenance fees. It's not just about opening the account; you need to manage your property taxes and garbage fees effectively once you own the place.
pixel_wanderer

I had luck with DSK Bank, but the paperwork requirements were strict. You will need your passport, a copy of the property deed or purchase contract, and sometimes proof of address in your home country.

Honestly, the banks change their internal policies on non-residents frequently. If one branch says no, try a different branch of the same bank. It's often down to the individual branch manager.

The lawyer route is the way to go. They can verify that the property has no outstanding debts, which is much more important than just the bank account itself.
