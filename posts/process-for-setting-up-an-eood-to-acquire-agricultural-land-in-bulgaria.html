Process for setting up an EOOD to acquire agricultural land in Bulgaria

stellar_byte
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.01.2025 10:52
I am looking to purchase several plots of agricultural land and gardens in Bulgaria and have been advised that opening an EOOD (Single-Member Limited Liability Company) is the best legal route for a non-resident. Could someone walk me through the necessary steps for registration, including the requirements for the Bulgarian trade register? Additionally, are there any specific restrictions or ongoing tax obligations I should be aware of once the land is under the company's name? Any advice or recommendations for local legal representation would be greatly appreciated.

Setting up an EOOD is definitely the standard route for non-residents to bypass restrictions on land ownership. You will need a local lawyer to draft the articles of association and handle the notary process. Before you start, check out this guide on the process for opening a Bulgarian bank account, as you will need one to deposit the initial capital.
wild_flower_ink
Replied: 06.02.2025 00:13
The registration process in the Commercial Register takes about 3-5 business days once your documents are notarized and submitted. You will need a Bulgarian address for the company seat, which most law firms can provide as a virtual office service.
velvet_sky

Don't forget that once the land is in the company name, you are liable for annual corporate tax at 10% and local land taxes. It's a straightforward process, but keep a good accountant on retainer to handle the mandatory annual financial statements.

You will need a power of attorney for your local lawyer to represent you at the Commercial Register. It is much easier than flying back and forth to Sofia for every signature.

One thing people often overlook is the 'land category' status. Agricultural land in Bulgaria is categorized 1 through 10. If you want to change the status for construction later, it is much easier if the land is of a lower category (7-10).

Be prepared for the annual accounting fees. Even if the company is dormant, you still have to file a 'zero' report with the Trade Register every year. If you miss the deadline, the fines are quite steep.
