What to do if your developer goes bankrupt before completing the building?

cloud_mapper
Topic Starter
Posted: 08.01.2025 07:46
I recently received notice that my property developer has filed for bankruptcy, leaving my apartment unit only partially finished. I have already paid a significant deposit and progress payments, and I am terrified of losing my investment entirely. What are the immediate legal steps I should take to protect my interests? Are there any options to force the completion of the project through a new contractor or a receivership?

Replies (12)

stellar_byte
Replied: 11.01.2025 16:24
I am so sorry you are going through this. First, you absolutely need to file a formal claim with the bankruptcy trustee immediately. Do not miss the deadline, or you will lose your status as a creditor entirely.
blaze_horizon
Replied: 04.04.2025 12:53
You should contact a local property lawyer who specializes in insolvency. They can determine if you have a 'prior claim' on the property or if you are considered an unsecured creditor, which is a much worse position.
midnight_voyager
Replied: 25.04.2025 16:28
Check your purchase contract for any 'completion guarantees' or insurance policies. Sometimes these are mandatory, and they might provide a safety net if the developer goes under.

If you are looking at how to protect your assets or need to understand your financial standing, you might want to look into mortgage rates and bank loan options for foreign buyers in Bulgaria to see if you have any leverage with the banks involved in the project.
sage_whisper
Replied: 11.09.2025 05:58
Is there an active homeowners' association? Sometimes the buyers can band together to form a consortium to take over the project, but you need a majority vote and significant extra capital to hire a new contractor.
velvet_sky
Replied: 22.10.2025 07:44
Gather all your receipts, bank transfer records, and correspondence. You will need a clear paper trail to prove your interest in the property when the liquidator starts selling off assets.

Contact the other buyers. There is strength in numbers. If you all hire the same lawyer, the legal fees will be cheaper, and you will have a stronger voice when negotiating with the receivership.

Don't panic, but act fast. The bank that financed the construction usually has first priority on the land. You need to find out if the bank is going to step in and finish the building themselves.

Ask the liquidator if the project has a 'completion bond.' If it does, the insurance company might be obligated to pay for the remaining construction work.

It is a nightmare scenario. I would recommend documenting everything and checking if your contract allows for a clawback of payments made for unfinished works. It's a long shot, but worth checking with a specialist.
